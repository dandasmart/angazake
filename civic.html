💰 Finance Bill
The Finance Bill 2024 — What It Meant for Kenyans
The bill that sparked nationwide protests in June 2024 — explained simply for every citizen

The Finance Bill 2024 was a government proposal to introduce new taxes and increase existing ones to raise additional revenue for the government. It was introduced in Parliament in May 2024 by the National Treasury.

The bill proposed to raise KES 346 billion in additional taxes — money the government said it needed to pay debts and fund development.

⚠️ The bill was rejected by Kenyans through mass protests in June 2024 — one of the most significant citizen-led governance actions in Kenya's history.
  • Bread tax (16% VAT): Bread was to be taxed for the first time
  • Cooking oil tax (16% VAT): Edible oils were to be taxed
  • Financial transactions levy (0.05%): Tax on money transfers including M-Pesa
  • Motor vehicle circulation tax: Annual tax of KES 5,000–100,000 per vehicle
  • Eco levy: Tax on imported phones, laptops, diapers, sanitary pads
  • Sugar tax: Additional levy on sugar
These taxes would have hit ordinary Kenyans hardest — especially on everyday household items.

Young Kenyans — particularly Gen Z — organised nationwide protests through social media starting June 18, 2024. The protests were remarkable for several reasons:

  • They were leaderless — organised through WhatsApp, X (Twitter), and TikTok
  • Protestors occupied Parliament buildings on June 25, 2024
  • At least 39 people died and hundreds were injured in clashes with police
  • President Ruto declined to sign the bill on June 26, 2024
✅ Result: The Finance Bill 2024 was officially withdrawn. This proved that citizen action can directly change government policy.

The Finance Bill 2024 story teaches Masinga citizens several important lessons:

  • Citizens have the power to change government decisions when they act together
  • Every tax proposal in Parliament affects your daily life — bread, cooking oil, phone costs
  • You have a right to participate in public discussions on tax bills before they become law
  • Angazake's Parliament page tracks all current bills so you know what is coming

📊 National Budget
Kenya's 2025/2026 National Budget — What You Need to Know
Where your tax money goes — broken down simply for every Kenyan

The 2025/2026 National Budget is approximately KES 4.2 trillion. This is money the national government plans to spend between July 2025 and June 2026.

💡 KES 4.2 trillion is equivalent to every Kenyan receiving approximately KES 80,000 — yet many citizens feel nothing is being done in their area. Accountability is the missing link.
  • Tax revenue (KRA): Income tax (PAYE), VAT, excise duty, import duty
  • External loans: From World Bank, IMF, China, commercial banks
  • Grants: From donors and development partners
  • Internal borrowing: Government bonds and treasury bills
⚠️ Over 30% of Kenya's budget goes to debt repayment — meaning less money for roads, hospitals and schools.

Under Kenya's devolution system, counties receive an equitable share from the national government. Machakos County typically receives approximately KES 6–8 billion annually as its county allocation.

Additionally, Masinga Constituency receives a CDF allocation (approximately KES 100–120 million per year) managed by the CDF Board.

💡 Angazake tracks how every shilling of CDF and county funds is spent in Masinga Sub-County.

Kenya's budget process has several stages where citizens can participate:

  • Pre-budget public participation: County governments and Parliament hold meetings before the budget is tabled — you can attend and give views
  • Budget estimates review: After tabling, parliamentary committees review estimates and accept public submissions
  • CDF committee meetings: Your constituency has a CDF committee that holds meetings — attend and propose projects
  • County assembly debates: Watch your county assembly debate the county budget and engage your MCA

🏗️ CDF & Funds
How CDF Works — Your Constituency Development Fund Explained
Every constituency in Kenya gets CDF money. Here is how it works, who controls it, and how you benefit.

The Constituency Development Fund (CDF) is a fund established by law to channel government money directly to constituencies for development projects. It is managed separately from the county and national government budgets.

Masinga Constituency typically receives approximately KES 100–120 million per year. This is your money — as a citizen of Masinga, you have a right to know how it is spent.

✅ Angazake tracks all CDF projects in Masinga — roads, classrooms, dispensaries, water projects and more.
  • National CDF Board: Oversees all CDFs nationally
  • Constituency CDF Committee: A local committee that approves projects — includes the MP, community representatives, and officials
  • CDF Manager: A civil servant who manages day-to-day operations of the CDF office
  • The MP: Chairs the committee but cannot unilaterally approve or spend funds
⚠️ The MP does not own CDF money. It belongs to all citizens of the constituency.

Any community group, institution (school, hospital, church) or village can apply for CDF funding. The process:

  • Get an application form from the CDF office in Masinga or download online
  • Fill in the project details, cost estimate, and community need
  • Submit to the CDF committee — they meet regularly to review applications
  • If approved, a contractor is procured and the project begins
  • You can track approved projects on Angazake

Each of the 5 wards in Masinga Sub-County also receives a Ward Development Fund allocation from the Machakos County Government. This is separate from CDF and is managed by the MCA (Member of County Assembly) through the ward committee.

Ward funds typically range from KES 5–15 million per ward per year depending on county allocations.

⚖️ Citizen Rights
Your Rights as a Kenyan Citizen — The Constitution Made Simple
Kenya's 2010 Constitution gives you powerful rights. Here is what you need to know.

Article 35 of the Constitution gives every Kenyan citizen the right to access any information held by the State or any other person that is required for the exercise of any right or fundamental freedom.

✅ This means you have a legal right to ask for CDF records, ward fund expenditure, project contracts, and government spending in your area. If refused, you can go to court.

The Constitution requires Parliament and county assemblies to facilitate public participation before passing laws or making major decisions. You have the right to:

  • Be notified of public participation meetings
  • Attend and give your views
  • Submit written memoranda
  • Have your views considered (though not necessarily adopted)
💡 Angazake will publish all public participation notices for Machakos County so you never miss a meeting.

Any person has the right to petition Parliament to consider any matter within its authority — including demanding action on a stalled project, requesting an audit, or raising a governance concern.

You can also petition the county assembly for matters within county jurisdiction.

If you suspect corruption in a CDF, ward, county or national project:

  • Ethics and Anti-Corruption Commission (EACC): Report online at eacc.go.ke or call 0800 720 617 (toll free)
  • Director of Public Prosecutions (DPP): For serious corruption cases
  • Controller of Budget: For misuse of public funds — controller.go.ke
  • Angazake: Report the stalled or suspicious project on our platform — we verify and publish

🤝 Public Participation
How to Participate in Government Decisions — A Practical Guide
Government is required by law to involve you. Here is how to make your voice count.

Public participation is the process by which government — national, county, or parliament — invites citizens to give their views before making decisions, passing laws, or approving budgets. It is a constitutional requirement, not a favour.

💡 Any law passed without genuine public participation can be challenged in court and declared unconstitutional.

Public participation meetings happen at different levels:

  • National Parliament: Committee meetings open to public — advertised in newspapers and Parliament website
  • County Assembly: Machakos County Assembly holds PP meetings across the county — watch for announcements
  • Ward level: Your MCA sometimes holds ward meetings — follow their social media or contact their office
  • CDF: CDF committees hold community meetings to approve projects
📅 Angazake will publish all upcoming public participation notices for Masinga Sub-County and Machakos County.

A memorandum is a written submission of your views. You do not need a lawyer to write one. A simple memorandum should include:

  • Your name and contact details
  • The specific law or policy you are commenting on
  • What you support or oppose and why
  • What change you recommend
  • Your signature

Submit to the relevant committee clerk at Parliament or the county assembly speaker's office.

🏛️ Devolution
National vs County Government — Who Does What?
Understand who is responsible for which services so you know who to hold accountable.
  • Primary and secondary schools: Teachers (TSC), examinations (KNEC), curriculum (KICD)
  • National roads: KeRRA (rural), KURA (urban), KeNHA (highways)
  • Security: Police, prisons, courts, immigration
  • National hospitals: Kenyatta National Hospital, referral hospitals
  • Electricity: REA (rural electrification), KPLC (distribution)
  • CDF: Managed through the National CDF Board
  • County hospitals and health centres: Dispensaries, health centres, county referral hospitals
  • County roads: Grading, gravelling, murram roads within the county
  • Agriculture: Extension services, livestock, fisheries
  • Markets: County markets, trading licences
  • Water: County water supply and sanitation (in some areas)
  • Early childhood education: Pre-primary schools (ECD)
  • Ward Development Fund: Through MCAs
💡 If your dispensary has no drugs, hold the county government accountable — not the national government.
